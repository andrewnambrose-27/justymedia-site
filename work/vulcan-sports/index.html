Website design & development

My work brings the existing Vulcan Sports identity into the website’s design, content structure and development. The kit designer, club shops and ordering routes form part of the customer journey; this case study focuses on how the website presents and connects them.

The business challenge

One range.
Different starting points.

The website needed to serve clubs ready to create a kit as well as visitors still comparing ranges, lead times and manufacturing options. It also needed to retain the energy of a performance-sports brand.

Clubs, schools and organisations need different levels of support. The website has to connect the product choice with the next useful action, while making the manufacturing story easy to understand.

Design & development decisions

A clearer path through the offer.

01

Separate the routes into the range.

Distinct navigation and homepage cards lead to bespoke teamwear, traditional shirts, training and leisurewear, and club shops. Visitors can browse a range, find their club’s shop or review the brochure without following the same path.

02

Give different buyers a useful next step.

The kit designer and kit-package links serve visitors ready to explore a design. Brochure, support and enquiry routes give people who need more information a way to start a conversation.

03

Carry the identity through the details.

Charcoal panels, orange calls to action and product-led imagery carry Vulcan’s established identity through the page. Clear section headings give the product range and supporting information their own space.

The manufacturing story

From the factory to the customer.

The factory imagery and UK manufacturing section sit alongside the product offer, connecting what customers can order with the people and process behind it. The design-to-delivery sequence explains the steps from an initial idea through approval, production and delivery.

Delivered improvements

A connected presentation.

The delivered website brings the ranges, club-shop access, brochure, manufacturing story and direct contact routes into a consistent presentation. Product cards support browsing, while brochure and direct enquiry links give visitors ways to continue when they need help.

Ongoing improvement

Keep learning from the journey.

Further improvements can be guided by real enquiries and usage data: which ranges people look for, where they need help and how easily they reach the right next step. Product content, support guidance and enquiry routes are useful areas to review as the site develops.
